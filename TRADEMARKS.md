The MIT License covers the **code**. It grants **no** trademark or branding
rights. A trademark is about identifying the source of the software, not about
who may use the code.

## You may (without asking)

- Use the name "botpolice" to **refer to the project** factually, in articles,
  talks, tutorials, and comparisons.
- **Link** to the official repository at
  https://github.com/TheDub-lab/botpolice.
- State that your product or project **"works with"**, **"integrates with"**, or
  **"is based on"** botpolice, provided the statement is accurate and does not
  imply endorsement or sponsorship.
- Redistribute the unmodified open core under the MIT License while keeping its
  notices intact.

## You may not (without written permission)

- Use the Marks in the **name** of a product, service, company, domain, or
  distribution that is not the official one.
- Use the logo or branding in a way that **implies sponsorship, affiliation, or
  endorsement** by TheDub-lab.
- Distribute a **modified** version of the software under the "botpolice" name.
- Register or attempt to register any mark confusingly similar to the Marks.

## Forks and redistributions

If you fork or redistribute the software:

1. **Rename** your project so it does not use the "botpolice" name or logo.
2. **Remove** the official branding (logo, name, and any references that imply
   it is the official project).
3. You **may** include an accurate attribution such as *"based on botpolice"*
   with a link to the official repository.
